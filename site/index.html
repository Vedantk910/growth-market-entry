How I built it — step by step

  1. Frame one decision. "Should a last-mile operator enter e-commerce fulfillment?" reduced to three strategies priced on one yardstick (USD/parcel net contribution): enter in-house, partner (lease a 3PL's capacity), or decline. Last-mile → e-comm was the chosen variant — a defensible "I know this industry" pick (an in-house school operating model + a retail-risk background).
  2. Sourcing — public numbers only, each tagged. Top-down: Menzies Parcel Economy (124.9B US parcels, EST) and the filed USPS commercial-package figure (53.5B, 2023 — CONFIRMED, the independent anchor). Bottom-up: USPS, UPS and RRD filings (cost/parcel derived, tagged) plus RRD's +41% fulfillment volume growth as the adjacent-market entry case. No number on the page is untagged.
  3. Model — pure functions, closed forms first. The flip volume is one line of algebra, V* = F/(p − cv); the 5-yr PV break-even is a ratio of discounted annuity sums, pinned against a binary-search sweep to 1e-6. No black-box numerics anywhere in the path a client would re-run.
  4. Tests — 33 offline pytest cases. Each module carries a closed-form-vs-numeric pin, a scale/decay invariance check, and a sensitivity check on the pivot inputs. Zero network, deterministic, one-command re-run.
  5. The proof that makes it a tool, not a description. An offline cross-check proves the trigger is invariant to every market-size factor (halve the SAM, the flip is bit-identical) while every pivot input moves it. That ablation is the answer to "your market number is a range — which number does the decision rest on?".
  6. Reproduce in one command. python notebooks/run_notebook.py regenerates out/decision_numbers.json; GitHub Actions runs the suite + the notebook on every push; this page re-implements the same closed forms in JS and a Node harness pins the two languages to the same bits.
  7. The hostile pass. Three interviewer attacks written down first, each answered with a number, not an appeal — see the panel below.

The three hostile attacks, answered

"Your market number is a range — which number does the decision actually rest on?"

Not the SAM. The decision rests on the flip F/(p−cv) and the decline rate R − floor — closed forms in cost and the partner rate. The repo's offline cross-check halve-proves it: the trigger is bit-identical when the SAM is halved, and the SAM only sets headroom (54× the flip at baseline).

"The partner rate and hub cost are two numbers you picked — the whole thing is one parameter."

Two inputs, isolated by the formula: the client sees exactly how much each is worth, and the 72-cell cost × rate surface is the partner screenshot — the trigger moves continuously across the band, which is the re-runnable part. Build / lease / decline are priced on one yardstick (USD/parcel net contribution), not three gut calls.

"RRD's $223/parcel says nothing about last-mile cost — your bottom-up bench is broken."

It is tagged, and the tag does the work: the per-parcel figures are derived (revenue ÷ volume), not filed, and the confidence tag carries that. RRD anchors the fulfillment margin and the adjacent-entry question; it does not anchor delivery unit cost — which is why the decision pivot is the committee's cost-to-serve, not the filing.
